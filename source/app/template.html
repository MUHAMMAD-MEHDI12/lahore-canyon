Data & method

How the Lahore Canyon is built

Two 1.2 × 1.2 km tiles on a 4 m grid: the Walled City around Rang Mahal, and DHA Phase 2. Every exposed wall is cut into storey-high panels, and every roof and street cell is a surface. For each surface the model computes how much sky it sees, whether it is in direct sun each hour (by marching rays across the 3D grid), and a surface energy balance with a thermal-mass lag. The what-if scenarios rerun that balance in your browser.

Data

  • Building heights: Google Open Buildings 2.5D Temporal, 2023 layer, 4 m effective resolution. Dataset
  • Surface temperature and vegetation: Landsat 8 Collection 2 Level-2 (ST_B10, red, NIR), scene 149/038, 7 Jun 2026 10:35 PKT, via Microsoft Planetary Computer. Dataset
  • Hourly weather (air temperature, humidity, direct and diffuse sun, wind, boundary-layer height): ERA5 reanalysis via the Open-Meteo archive API. API
  • PM2.5, PM10, NO₂: CAMS air-quality model via Open-Meteo. These are modelled estimates, not station measurements. API
  • Landmark names: © OpenStreetMap contributors. Licence

Both agree the Walled City runs hotter at mid-morning: by 1.5 °C in Landsat and 2.2 °C in the model. The spatial pattern inside each tile is only weakly captured (r ≈ 0.4): Landsat's thermal band is 100 m native, coarser than the features that drive the model.

Limits

  • Air temperature and wind come from one 9 km ERA5 cell, the same for both tiles. The model maps surface heat, not street-level air temperature.
  • Heights are machine-learning estimates. Lanes narrower than about 4 m are not resolved, and faces snap to four compass directions.
  • Satellite checks are daytime only. Night-time figures are model results.
  • Street ventilation is a geometric index (sky view), not a pollution simulation. PM2.5 and the mixing-layer height are city-scale values.
  • Floor space stands in for residents; no population layer was used.
